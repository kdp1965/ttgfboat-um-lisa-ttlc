# LISA on GF180: the port of the ttsky26d chip to the Tiny Tapeout "Best Of All Time" shuttle

The RTL is the third LISA chip's, ttsky26d-um-lisa-ttlc at rc30 (commit
cb0cb58, 2026-10-27: RESPIN.md items 1-39, REV3.md), with everything it
has - the 24-bit core, the BF16 unit, the divider, the TTLC, the data cache,
the streaming / DDR fetch, the peripherals - and without Calvin.  The
target is the GF180 PDK (gf180mcuD, the 7-track 5 V library
gf180mcu_fd_sc_mcu7t5v0 run at 3.3 V) through the ttgf26c tooling:
tt-gds-action's custom_gds / precheck / viewer at that branch, the gf180mcuD
tile templates, LibreLane 3.0.x with open_pdks 54435919 (the version the
action enables).  This file is the port's log; RESPIN.md stays the
design's history.

## What changed for GF180

* **The two hand-instantiated cells.**  `lisa_dbg.v` and `debug_regs.v`
  keep the breakpoint and the static debug registers in latches gated by a
  strong AND of the write strobe: sky130's `dlrtp_1` (RESET_B, GATE, D, Q)
  and `and2_4` (A, B, X) are GF180's `latrnq_1` (RN, E, D, Q) and `and2_4`
  (A1, A2, Z).  The power pins under `USE_POWER_PINS` are the GF180 cells'
  VDD / VSS / VNW / VPW, tied to the design's VPWR / VGND nets (LibreLane's
  gf180mcuD names: the top module and the final netlist carry VPWR / VGND,
  the cells' VDD / VSS are connected to them).  The GF180 cell models
  declare those pins `inout`, and Verilator (the flow's lint, with the PDK
  models) refuses an inout driven from an `input` port, so the
  `USE_POWER_PINS` ports of tt_um_pettit_lisa, lisa_core, lisa_dbg,
  debug_regs and the RAM32 model are `inout` now.
* **The behavioural models** of the two cells for the RTL simulation
  (test/test_cells.v) and the FPGA (fpga/arty_a7/fpga_cells.v,
  latch2flop.py) follow.
* **lisa.sdc**'s register search matches GF180's `dffq` / `latq` library
  cell names as well as sky130's `dfxtp` / `dlxtp` (`*df*`, `*dl*`,
  `*lat*`).
* **src/config.json** is the GF180 template's (ttgf-verilog-template)
  with the chip's additions: lisa.sdc, AREA synthesis, the delay cells kept
  from the resizer (`dlya_*` .. `dlyd_*`: ~4 ns each, as the Tiny Galton
  and PRISM ports found), the clock period for GF180.  No
  SYNTH_EXCLUDED_CELL_FILE: GF180's `no_synth.cells` excludes only the scan
  flops.
* **harden/** is the sky130 flow less the register anchoring and the chip
  art: configure.py builds the configuration the way `tt_tool.py
  --create-user-config --gf` does (the tile from tt-support-tools'
  tech/gf180mcuD/tile_sizes.yaml and def/ - copies are here - and the
  3.3 V corners: LIB, STA_CORNERS, DEFAULT_CORNER, LIB_SYNTH / FASTEST /
  SLOWEST, VDD_PIN_VOLTAGE, which LibreLane's gf180mcuD defaults leave at
  5 V), and the Makefile names the PDK version directory: the ciel symlink
  in ~/projects/fossi pointed at a newer open_pdks (1689ac3f) whose
  LibreLane configuration this LibreLane (3.0.2) reads with an empty LIB -
  synthesis then has no cell library and the hand-instantiated cells are
  "not part of the design".
* **info.yaml**: the 4x4 tile (1440.32 x 736.96 um, the largest a GF180
  project may take; the sky130 chip was a 5x4, 856.52 x 511.36), the
  clock provisional.
* **The workflows**: gds.yaml publishes the locally hardened GDS with
  custom_gds@ttgf26c (pdk gf180mcuD), then precheck and the viewer at the
  same branch; docs.yaml the same; test.yaml unchanged (the RTL bench).

## What is not on the GF180 chip

* **The BF16 unit** (Ken, 2026-10-27: the biggest block that could go -
  114K um2 synthesized, 16% of the design, the lampFPU divider 81.5K of
  it).  `WANT_BF16` is undefined in lisa_core.v; the unit's sources
  (fops.v, lampFPU_div_top.sv, lampFPU_div.sv, lampFPU_fractDiv.sv,
  lampFPU_utils.v, lampFPU.h) and its bench (test/fops/) are not in this
  repository - they are in ttsky26d-um-lisa-ttlc, and the define is the
  switch.  The core without the define had six references to the unit
  outside its `ifdef`s (op_fops and op_fdiv_r in the second-stage and
  interrupt logic, op_fcmp_r in the carry and zero flag loads, fdiv_valid
  / fdiv_ready in the exec state's wait): an `else` branch declares those
  five as constants (no float op, the divide never waited on) and the
  fcmp arms are inside the `ifdef`.  The BF16 opcodes (tfa, taf, fmul,
  fdiv, fadd, fneg, fswap, itof, ftoi, fclr, fcmp) decode to nothing and
  execute as nops.  test_bf16 and test_bf16f are out of test/run_suite.sh's
  list; sdcc -mlisa's float library (bf16_add and the --bf16-float
  arithmetic) uses the instructions, so C programs with floats do not run
  on this chip.
* **The TTLC** (Ken, 2026-10-27, after the 4x4 did not route with it: the
  MC14500B-compatible logic controller, its 74HC595 / 74HC165 scan chains,
  its interlock with the SPI master, its debugger view).  `WANT_TTLC` is
  undefined in tt_um_pettit_lisa.v: the mc14500b and shift_reg_io
  instances, the scan interlock and the ICU's connections go (mc14500b.v
  and shift_reg_io.v, the TTLC programs and tests are not in this
  repository).  What the WS2812B driver uses stays: ttlc_io's 48 output
  flops as its pixel store, written through lisa_ttlcio's TIDX / TDATA
  window (bytes 0-5; the other bytes read 0 and the inputs and the storage
  are not there), and ttlc_fetch as its DMA engine (fetch_en is the DMA
  request alone; the fetch address is the data select's).  The ICU's
  inputs to those two modules are tied to constants and its outputs left
  unconnected, so synthesis removes the slot machinery and the inputs.
  The 64-bit storage did not go with them (the core wrote it and read it
  back through bytes 12-15 and 20-23, so it was live: 64 flops and their
  byte muxes, with Port D to LISA and the TTLC interrupt behind bits 0-8 -
  a write to bit 8 was a software interrupt); `WANT_TTLC` in ttlc_io.v
  removes it too (2026-10-27, Ken: 8 bytes behind an indexed window are no
  use beside the 128-byte direct RAM): the macro-flow synthesis went from
  451.3K to 447.9K um2 (-3.3K, 2063 -> 1997 flops; the standard flow with
  the RAM synthesized in, 575.4K -> 568.4K), the bench's DEFAULT / WS2812 /
  WSDMA groups pass.  The ICU's debug registers went next (Ken, the same
  evening; `WANT_TTLC` in debug_regs.v): 0x40 run control, 0x41 PC, the
  three breakpoints 0x48-0x4a (45 latches), the TTLC base 0x1f (16
  latches) and the TTLC fetch's select in 0x15's upper bits (the fetch is
  the DMA's and uses lisa2's select) - they read 0, ttlc_halt is 1; the
  macro-flow synthesis went 447.9K -> 438.3K um2 (231 -> 170 latches,
  1997 -> 1990 flops), the bench's DEFAULT / WS2812 / WSDMA groups pass.
  The SPI master's claim is granted at once (no scan ever holds it);
  TCTRL's START does nothing.
* **The watchdog** (Ken, 2026-10-28, after gf5-gf7 did not route:
  RESPIN 36's lisa_wdt.v, 6.6K um2 synthesized).  `WANT_WDT` is undefined
  in lisa_periph.v: 0x4C-0x4F read 0 and take no writes, `wdt_reset` is 0;
  lisa_wdt.v, the source lists and test/progs/misc36.c's watchdog checks
  are gone (the program checks the registers read 0 instead).  Macro-flow
  synthesis 438.3K -> 434.7K um2 (1990 -> 1943 flops); the bench's DEFAULT
  / MISC36 / WS2812 / WSDMA groups pass.
* **Calvin** (the placement-blockage art of the sky130 chip).

## Verification

The chip bench (test/) with the GF180 cell models: the default suite,
TIMER3, WS2812, WSDMA, SPIM, TTLCIO, OVERRUN, I2C, MISC36, TTLC and
DDRSWEEP pass under Verilator, the default suite under icarus; the REV3
SDCC suite 28 of 28 on the chip bench and 27 of 27 on the core bench
(2026-10-27).  No RTL other than the two cell instantiations and the power
port directions changed.

(nand2_1 3.75 -> 10.98 um2, a flop 20.0 -> 63.7, a latch 15.0 -> 43.9,
and2_4 8.8 -> 37.3).  Synthesis of the whole design (AREA 1, the RAM32 as
flops): 29919 cells, 725768 um2 of standard cells, 3899 flops; the
sky130 chip's synthesis without the RAM32 was 25173 cells, 187848 um2.

**Without the BF16 unit** (below): 24124 cells, 610760 um2, 3621 flops.
**Without the TTLC as well**: 22554 cells, 575426 um2, 3465 flops - the cut
took 35.4K where the MC14500B and the scan chains were 10.5K, so about 25K
of ttlc_io and ttlc_fetch went with the ICU's constants (synthesis is
flat), and about 30K of them stays: the 48 pixel flops and their TIDX /
TDATA write and read, the DMA engine with its two 48-bit group buffers and
QSPI handshake.

**What the tile holds.**  The 4x4 tile's core is 1433.6 x 729.1 um =
1045K um2.  On GF180 the flow's buffers, the hold fixes and CTS add
43-50% to the synthesized area (Ken's two BOAT projects: Tiny Galton
85.0K -> 121.3K, PRISM 60.4K -> 90.9K; the sky130 chip grew 28%), so the
whole design would place at about 1040-1090K um2 of standard cells: the
tile is full before routing, against the 64.6% the sky130 chip (its 5x4
tile) routed at with difficulty.  Without the BF16 unit and the TTLC
(-182K synthesized) it would still be about 75%; the RAM32 as a DFFRAM
macro trades 123.5K x 1.45 of placed flops and muxes for the macro's
footprint (below).  A LISA without BF16 and TTLC, the RAM in a macro, is
about 62% of the 4x4; with Timer 3 back 65%.

## The RAM32 macro

The 128-byte RAM is a DFFRAM macro, as on the sky130 chips: 1024 latches
(latq_1) written through 128 clock gates, read through the AND-OR path
Ken built for the sky130 rc5 (DFFRAM.librelane models/ram_ao: a nand2
beside every latch, nand4s per 8-word slice, or4s per 16-word block, the
output register and a 2:1 mux - nothing floats, no tri-state bus), built
with `python3 dffram.py --pdk gf180mcuD -b ram_ao -C 30 32x32` in
DFFRAM.librelane (its products copied to macros/).  What the GF180 port of
DFFRAM needed (2026-10-27, in DFFRAM.librelane):

* platforms/gf180mcuD's block_definitions.v had no AND-OR blocks: BYTE_AO
  and WORD_AO from the sky130 ones with GF180's cells (latq_1 / dffq_1,
  nand2_1, inv_2, the icgtp_1 clock gate, buf_2, and2_1, the antenna
  diode), and NAND4 / OR4 wrapper modules, which models/ram_ao's model.v
  now instantiates in place of the sky130 nand4_1 / or4_1 (the sky130A and
  sky130B platforms got the same wrappers, so one model serves all three).
* Its tech.yml listed the 5 V libraries (and the slow one misspelt): the
  3.3 V ones, plus a flow_config with the Tiny Tapeout GF180 tile's stripe
  grid (FP_PDN_VOFFSET 16.32 / VPITCH 38.87 on Metal4), Metal3 as the
  route-through layer (the sky130 platform's met3), the 3.3 V corners
  (LIB / STA_CORNERS / DEFAULT_CORNER, so the macro's .lib views carry the
  parent's corner names), and VPWR / VGND as the power pin names (the PDK's
  defaults are VDD / VSS; LibreLane's gf180mcuD top level, the tile and
  the parent flow say VPWR / VGND).
* The CLKBUF_16 wrapper is a clkbuf_8: GF180's clkbuf_16 is 50 sites
  (109.8 um2) and the RAM32's 32 Di buffers sit in one row, which was
  896 um wide and set the macro's width at 946.96 x 168.56 um = 159.6K
  um2; the latch rows are about 600 um.  With clkbuf_8 (26 sites): 675.92
  x 168.56 um = 113.9K um2, 41 rows (32 words, 4 of nand4s / or4s, the
  output register, the buffers and muxes), no spare or decap rows (Ken's
  packed placer of the sky130 rc5).  The stock models/ram RAM32 (bufz_1
  tri-state read path) on GF180 was 963.2 x 160.72 um and its detailed
  route was still at 1-2 violations after 30 iterations; the AND-OR one
  routed clean at the router's first pass.

The macro: routing DRC 0, Magic DRC 0, LVS clean; 2 antenna violations
(two address-buffer nets in the slice decoders on Metal3, ratios 1.09 and
1.34; the model's flow repairs with jumpers only, see models/ram_ao's
config.yml); timing at 30 ns: every violator is an input-to-register path
(the address into the write clock gates, the data into the latches), which
the parent's STA constrains through the macro's .lib - no internal
register-to-register violation.  Its 17 VPWR and 17 VGND Metal4 stripes
are 38.87 um apart from 17.44 um (VPWR) and 20.74 um (VGND) off its
origin, so placed with its origin x at 2.24 + k x 38.87 um they lie on the
tile's.  Pins: Di0 north on Metal2, Do0 west and the controls east on
Metal3 (the sky130 macro's sides, for the same corner of the tile).

## Hardening

`make -C harden macro` (the README's Building): LibreLane's Classic flow
with the RAM32 macro and Tile.ExtendPowerStripes (the tile's Metal4
stripes drawn full height over the macro's pin columns, 17 of them, all
on the tile grid at x 740.77 / 1090.60), on gf180mcuD 54435919, the 3.3 V
corners, CLOCK_PERIOD 40 ns.

* **gf1, the 4x4 tile** (2026-10-27): the flow's buffers took the
  synthesized 487K um2 (610.8K less the RAM32's flops) to 692K of placed
  cells, 77.1% of the core beside the macro; the global router's overflow
  was 24632 (the sky130 rc23: 830) and the detailed router went 64K ->
  41.8K -> 36.7K -> 33.7K violations in its first three iterations (the
  sky130 runs are under 1000 by the tenth).  Stopped; Ken: a 5x4 tile.
* **A 5x4 tile** was tried for an hour (gf2: a 4x4 template widened by a
  tile column, 1804.88 x 736.96 um) and withdrawn: the 4x4 is the largest
  tile the GF180 shuttle offers.  Ken: drop the TTLC instead, keeping what
  the WS2812B driver uses (above).
* **gf3, the 4x4 without the TTLC** (2026-10-27): the core logic 452K um2
  synthesized (575.4K less the RAM32's flops), about 642K placed by gf1's
  1.42x, 69% of the 929K um2 beside the macro - the floor for the
  placer's target density.  The sky130 chip's recipe: GPL_CELL_PADDING 2
  (a site each side of every cell in global placement, +16% at GF180's
  6.9 um average cell) with the target at the padded utilization plus
  slack.  gf3 was lost to a wrong macro location (x 1090.60, the 5x4's,
  left in macro.json by an edit that changed only its comment: 326 um of
  the macro outside the die, the router's "26 pins outside die area", and
  the placer leaving the bottom-left corner empty - Ken saw the same empty
  regions beside the macro in gf1).  The target: GPL's "minimum feasible
  density" is the padded cell area over the free area, about 60% here
  (481K um2 of cells at global placement, 52% of the 929K beside the
  macro, 1.16x padded), and a target far above it leaves the far corner
  empty while the rest packs - so 72, a little above, for gf4.
* **gf4** (2026-10-27): placed at 72 with the padding (GPL took 0.72,
  0.73 in its routability phase); the cells came to 632.7K um2 after the
  repairs, 68% of the area beside the macro; global-route overflow 12887;
  the detailed router 29.8K violations at its first iteration, 1394 at the
  20th, then between 1000 and 2000 to the 61st (1493) - stopped (Ken).
  The growth from the 451K synthesized: taps and end caps +29K, the slew /
  fanout repair +125K (4777 buffers in 1417 nets, 1858 upsizes: 2365 slew
  and 601 fanout violations at GF180's default fanout 10 and 3 ns
  transition limit), CTS +14K, the post-CTS timing repair +13K (276
  upsizes, 64 buffers; the slow corner from -48.7 to -28 ns at 40 ns - a
  ~68 ns path there, 12-15 MHz; the nominal corner -4.5 ns before that
  repair), hold 22 buffers.  The repair is the lever left in the flow:
  gf5 tries fanout 20 and 5 ns (harden/trial_repair.json).  gf5 (fanout 20 / transition 5 as an overlay, otherwise gf4's recipe)
placed at 548.8K um2 of standard cells = 59% beside the macro: the
post-placement repair added 45K (gf4: 125K; 1515 buffers / 798 resizes
against 4777 / 1858), CTS 15K, the timing repair 9K; nominal WNS +2.9 ns
at 40 ns after the repair, the slow corner -18.9 ns on the same path.
Its detailed router still plateaued, at about 1000 violations over 100+
iterations (stopped at 1160).  **Those were not density.**  The DRC report
(`make status`): 775 of 891 violations were shorts between top-level
Metal3 routes and `ram1`, *inside* the macro's outline (x 750-990,
y 20-172 of the macro at x 740.77-1416.69, y 3.92-172.48), on nets to its
Di pins (`i_data_cache.ram_di[*]`).  The macro's LEF was the DFFRAM
flow's route-through abstract (`LEF_ROUTE_THROUGH_LAYERS: [Metal3]`, as
the sky130 platform's met3): its Metal3 obstruction was the drawn
geometry, 16033 slivers, so the global router saw free Metal3 tracks over
the macro and sent nets through it; but about half of the macro's Metal3
shapes sit between the parent's tracks (the same tracks.info, Metal3 at
0.28 + k x 0.56, and the macro's y 3.92 is 7 pitches - 6150 of its 12000
horizontal wires are at the half-pitch phase, and 4400 via pads are 0.56
or 0.84 um wide), so the detailed router could not use those tracks and
shorted.  The fix is the macro's blanket abstract: Magic's own LEF
(`34-magic-writelef` of the DFFRAM run; pins identical, Metal1 / Metal2 /
Metal4 obstructions identical, Metal3 83 rectangles with 0.3 um around the
43 Metal3 pins) is now `macros/RAM32.lef`; the DFFRAM platform's
`LEF_ROUTE_THROUGH_LAYERS` is empty.  Nothing needs to cross a corner
macro, so the blanket costs no routing; the Metal4 stripes over it are the
power pins as before.  gf6 = gf5's recipe + this LEF + the RTL without the
TTLC storage and debug registers (438.3K synthesized).

gf6 placed at 532.8K um2 of standard cells (57% beside the macro; nominal
WNS +2.4 ns) and the macro-interior shorts were gone (71 violations
touching ram1 against gf5's 775), but the router still plateaued: 631 at
iteration 17, then the ripup rounds climbed (565 -> 949 -> 1162 -> 1621 ->
